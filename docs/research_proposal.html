Research Proposal · Draft

Redocking LSD

A reproducible docking ranking for psilocin at 5-HT2A doesn't mean the pipeline found the right pose. Here's what the receptor's own bound ligand says when you ask it to prove that.

Fabian Alvarez‑Primo · psilocin‑5ht2a · sert‑s438t‑escitalopram · 2026‑08‑31

Abstract

A rigid AutoDock Vina screen of six serotonergic ligands — LSD, psilocybin, serotonin, 5‑MeO‑DMT, psilocin, DMT — against 5‑HT2A (PDB 6WGT) produces a highly reproducible affinity ranking: five‑seed SD ≤0.05 kcal/mol per ligand, an order of magnitude below Vina's typical ∼0.5 kcal/mol noise floor. But self‑redocking 6WGT's own co‑crystallized ligand — 7LD, confirmed against RCSB's own entry to be LSD (Kim et al., Cell 2020, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2020.08.024), not 25‑CN‑NBOH as an earlier project note mis‑cited it — into the same box reproduces its blind‑screen affinity almost exactly (−10.073 vs. −10.1 kcal/mol) while missing its crystal pose by 5.11 Å RMSD (seed 42, reproducible), a clear fail against the standard 2.0 Å benchmark. Reproducibility and pose accuracy have come apart. This proposal lays out the flexible‑docking and MD work needed to determine whether the psilocybin > psilocin ordering — and the ranking generally — reflects real binding energetics or a rigid‑receptor artifact.

Background

Psilocin — psilocybin's active, dephosphorylated metabolite — and LSD are both 5‑HT2A agonists whose subjective effects can be blunted by SSRI pretreatment, a clinically observed but mechanistically underspecified interaction between presynaptic reuptake blockade (SERT) and postsynaptic agonism (5‑HT2A). The sibling project sert‑s438t‑escitalopram found the same class of gap on the SERT side: rigid docking failed to reproduce a 320‑fold experimental Ki loss from the S438T mutation (ΔΔG +0.269 kcal/mol, within noise), which motivated a flexible/MD roadmap there. This project asks the analogous question on the 5‑HT2A side — with a more direct validation lever available, since 6WGT's own bound ligand happens to be one of the six comparators. sert‑s438t‑escitalopram's own redocking benchmark (68P into 5I6Z) fails the same 2.0 Å convention (6.63 Å) — the score/pose dissociation shows up independently in both projects, weak but real evidence this is a property of single‑structure rigid Vina docking on a GPCR orthosteric pocket generally, not an artifact specific to either receptor's prep.

Preliminary data

Pipeline

6WGT (cryo‑EM) required a non‑trivial prep path: the BRIL fusion — a single‑chain, in‑frame replacement of ICL3, PDB‑renumbered 1001–1106 — had to be located by walking Cα residue numbers rather than trusting SEQRES; roughly 80 truncated side chains needed PDBFixer repair; and meeko's mk_prepare_receptor (strict RDKit‑template matching) could not parse the repaired structure at all, failing on an AtomValenceException at the true C‑terminus. ADFRsuite's prepare_receptor — geometry‑based bond building, no template matching — succeeded where meeko didn't: a fallback that should generalize to other moderate‑resolution cryo‑EM GPCR structures meeko chokes on.

Correction

An earlier project note mislabeled 6WGT's co‑crystallized ligand 7LD as 25‑CN‑NBOH. Its HETNAM record reads "lysergic acid diethylamide" — 7LD is LSD. That makes the redocking test below a genuine self‑redock: the validation ligand is also one of the six screened comparators.

Self‑redocking validation

7LD's crystal pose was extracted directly from the raw 6WGT coordinates and redocked into the same box used for the screen above.

Key finding

Affinity reproduced, pose missed. Top redocked pose (seed 42, reproducible): −10.073 kcal/mol — matching the blind‑screen LSD score almost exactly. RMSD to the crystal pose: 5.11 Å, against the standard 2.0 Å pass threshold. Per‑atom deviation was checked across all 24 heavy atoms to rule out a symmetric‑group RMSD artifact (LSD's diethylamide has two chemically equivalent ethyl arms); deviation was roughly uniform, 3.15–8.10 Å, not concentrated in two or three atoms — consistent with a genuinely different bound orientation, not a matching artifact.

The problem this proposal addresses

Two results that would normally corroborate each other have split apart. A reproducible ranking is usually read as evidence a docking protocol is “working.” Here, the same protocol, applied to the one ligand where ground truth exists, converges on the right affinity magnitude through the wrong geometry. The ranking's reproducibility can't be used as indirect evidence of its correctness — the two have to be established independently, and right now only one is.

Specific aims

1

Determine whether flexibility recovers the crystal pose

Re‑run the 7LD self‑redock with side‑chain flexibility enabled around the orthosteric residues already flagged as pocket‑adjacent (A:89, 135, 136, 146, 147, 348, 350, 351, 356), and/or against a small ensemble of receptor conformers rather than the single rigid 6WGT structure.

Pass criterion: redocked RMSD <2.0 Å for at least one flexible run or ensemble member.

Tooling note: the direct route — a Vina flexible‑residue PDBQT split — is currently blocked on this structure: the hcc conda build of ADFRsuite used for receptor prep doesn't ship prepare_flexreceptor, and meeko's --flexres mode still template‑matches the whole polymer first and fails on one of the exact nine target residues (ILE A:135), independent of the earlier C‑terminus failure. Hand‑authoring the torsion trees was considered and rejected — a wrong chi‑angle branch is silently invalid chemistry. Ensemble docking against MD‑generated conformers is the primary planned route, folding this aim into Aim 2's MD work.

2

MD‑validate the top psilocin pose; re‑examine the psilocybin ordering

Embed the best psilocin pose in a POPC bilayer, run 100–200 ns with GPU offload, track RMSD/RMSF and the Asp3.32 salt bridge plus TM5 Ser/His contacts. Run MM‑GBSA rescoring for psilocin and psilocybin on trajectory frames to test whether the phosphate‑driven psilocybin advantage survives a flexible, solvated environment.

3

Signaling‑bias proxy (stretch)

Compare TM6 outward displacement in the psilocin trajectory against a Gq‑biased agonist reference structure, as a rough active‑state conformation proxy.

Significance

  • Clinical — a structurally grounded account of psilocin's 5‑HT2A engagement is a prerequisite for reasoning about SSRI–psychedelic interactions, the question motivating the SERT/5‑HT2A pairing.
  • Scientific — a same‑structure ground‑truth case, rather than an assumed one, showing rigid‑docking reproducibility is not a reliable proxy for pose correctness in this receptor class.
  • Methodological — the ADFR receptor‑prep fallback and the paired multi‑seed/self‑redock validation harness, including a --strict gate mode that fails the pipeline non‑zero rather than silently logging a warning, are reusable directly (already shared with sert‑s438t‑escitalopram) by anyone hitting meeko's template‑matching wall on a moderate‑resolution structure.

Limitations

  • Single starting receptor conformation (6WGT only); no explicit waters or lipids in the current prep.
  • Vina's empirical scoring function is not re‑parameterized here for ergoline/tryptamine chemistry.
  • Six‑ligand comparator set is small and structurally related; no non‑binder negative control.
  • Psilocybin is a prodrug — its docking score describes a binding event unlikely to be physiologically relevant regardless of pose accuracy. Treated here as a known‑artifact calibration case for the flexible/MD pipeline, not as noise to discard.
